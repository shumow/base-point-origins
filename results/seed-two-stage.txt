Reading the stage-2 count: the limbs are matched as full 32-bit words,
0x0000003b (224/256k1, 26 leading zero bits) and 0x0554123b (192k1), each a
~2^-32 event; 655,216,640 checks give ~0.15 expected spurious per target.
So stage 2 is itself discriminating here, not a 1-in-64 nibble match, and a
hit would carry weight -- but it is only evidence on a seed that ALSO clears
stage 1 (a JOINT hit). A narrower low-6-bit reading of 0x3b was deliberately
not used: at 1/64 it would carry no information.

CONCLUSION
  No candidate seed clears both stages, and no seed reproduces a full value
  as a window of one wide hash. Stage 1 is 0 hits (as in §4.1), so the
  seed-input two-stage construction has no seed to stand on in this corpus;
  and stage 2's 32-bit limbs went unseen across 655M checks (~0.3 expected).
  With §7.3.8 and §7.3.9, the counter-before-hash family is now closed over every
  testable seed/digest input. A seed OUTSIDE this corpus (a long or machine
  input) stays beyond reach by construction -- stage 1 already said a short
  human string is not it. The high material still reads as a hand tag (§7.3.7).
