Reading the stage-2 count: the limbs are matched as full 32-bit words,
0x0000003b (224/256k1, 26 leading zero bits) and 0x0554123b (192k1), each a

So stage 2 is itself discriminating here, not a 1-in-64 nibble match, and a
hit would carry weight -- but it is only evidence on a seed that ALSO clears
stage 1 (a JOINT hit). A narrower low-6-bit reading of 0x3b was deliberately
not used: at 1/64 it would carry no information.

CONCLUSION
  No candidate seed clears both stages, and no seed reproduces a full value
  as a window of one wide hash. This is a bounded negative over an explicit
  domain: 13910 seed-encodings (the Certicom wordlist and the curves' p and n,
  encodings ['ascii', 'utf-16-le']), stage-1 hashes ['sha1', 'sha0', 'ripemd160', 'sha1(sha1)', 'sha1(md5)'] with the X9.62-style
  suffixes, stage-2 hashes ['sha1', 'md5', 'ripemd160', 'sha1(sha1)'], four placements, counters below
  256, 32-bit readings, and wide hashes ['sha256', 'sha512', 'sha1||sha1(x||1)']. It says nothing
  about seeds outside that corpus, other hashes, or larger counters. Stage 1
  alone is 0 hits here, as in §4.1. Together with §7.3.8 and §7.3.9 the
  seed-input two-stage construction is unsupported in every domain tested so
  far; the working hypothesis for the high material stays hand-assigned tags
  (§7.3.7), which these searches do not establish either.
