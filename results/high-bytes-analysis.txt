  secp160k1  48ce563f89a0ed9414f5aa28ad0d96d6795f9c62   (159 of 160 bits)
  secp192k1  554123b78ce563f89a0ed9414f5aa28ad0d96d6795f9c66   (187 of 192 bits)

1. Exact structure
  152-bit core (bits 4..155) identical on all four: True  (8ce563f89a0ed9414f5aa28ad0d96d6795f9c6)
  top byte of the 160-bit block (bits 152..159): 160k1=0x48, 192k1=0x78, 224k1=0x78, 256k1=0x78
  material above bit 160:                        160k1=0x0, 192k1=0x554123b, 224k1=0x3b, 256k1=0x3b
  last nibble (bits 0..3):                       160k1=2, 192k1=6, 224k1=3, 256k1=3
  normalise top byte->0x78, last nibble->0: all four give the same 160-bit V: True
      V = 78ce563f89a0ed9414f5aa28ad0d96d6795f9c60
  => one 160-bit block V shared by all four; 192/224/256 prepend high
     material, 160k1 instead clears 0x30 from V's top byte. 224k1 and 256k1
     are byte-for-byte identical (same V, same high 0x3b, same last nibble 3).

2. The ordering obstruction (kills every truncate-one-constant story)
  secp160k1  field 160 bits, high material  0 bits (0x0)
  secp192k1  field 192 bits, high material 27 bits (0x554123b)
  secp224k1  field 224 bits, high material  6 bits (0x3b)
  secp256k1  field 256 bits, high material  6 bits (0x3b)
  secp192k1 carries MORE high material (27 bits) than secp224k1/secp256k1
  (6 bits) over a SMALLER field. So the four are not low windows of one long
  constant truncated to field size, nor a byte stream read longer for bigger
  fields. Any generator must explain why the 192-bit curve got the most.

3. FIPS 186 / X9.62 'verifiably random' field-element expansion
   Expansion of a seed S to a t-bit field element F(S,t):
     H0 = SHA1(S); W0 = rightmost (t-160*floor((t-1)/160)) bits of H0;
     Wi = SHA1(S+i); F = W0 || W1 || ... (W0 most significant).
   Prediction that MATCHES the data: for t in {192,224,256}, s=1, so the
   low 160 bits of F are W1 = SHA1(S+1) -- a block common to those three,
   exactly as 192/224/256 share V in their low 160 bits.
   Prediction that FAILS: the high field is the rightmost h bits of H0=SHA1(S),
   a full-weight hash slice; observed high material is tiny.
     secp192k1: h=32 -> expect ~32-bit random high field; observed 0x554123b (27 bits).
     secp224k1: h=64 -> expect ~64-bit random high field; observed 0x3b (6 bits).
     secp256k1: h=96 -> expect ~96-bit random high field; observed 0x3b (6 bits).
   Single-seed contradiction (192 vs 224): a common S would force
     rightmost 32 bits of H0 = 0x554123b  and
     rightmost 64 bits of H0 = 0x3b;
     but rightmost 64 bits already fix rightmost 32 bits = 0x3b != 0x554123b. Contradiction.
   Weight: a SHA-1 slice equalling a 6-bit value needs ~58 leading zero bits
   (probability ~2^-58); the high material is not hash output.
   => X9.62/FIPS expansion is excluded. (Confirms §7.3.2 by a second route;
      its matching low-160 prediction is why the idea looked plausible.)

4. The secp160k1 top-byte reduction 0x78 -> 0x48
   0x78 - 0x48 = 0x30;  0x78 ^ 0x48 = 0x30;  0x78 & ~0x30 = 0x48 (clear bits 4,5).
   0x78-topped value = 78ce563f89a0ed9414f5aa28ad0d96d6795f9c62
   it is < p(secp160k1): True  (so no reduction mod p forces 0x48)
   its bit length 159 == 0x48 value's bit length 159: True (so not a bit-length mask either)
   On-curve lift test on secp160k1 (does the value lift to a point? any point
   has order n here, h=1, so any lift is a legal x(G/2)):
     top byte 0x48: low nibbles that lift = [0, 1, 2, 3, 6, 12, 13, 15]  <- includes the published nibble 2
     top byte 0x78: low nibbles that lift = [0, 1, 3, 4, 5, 6, 8, 9, 10, 11, 14]
   Reading: both top bytes admit on-curve points, so 0x48 was not forced by
   lifting. The 0x30 clear is a deliberate 2-bit edit to V's top byte, applied
   only on the one curve that carries no high material. No field-level cause.

5. Is the high material a counter, date, or function of the curve parameters?
   values in generation-size order: 160k1->(none), 224k1->0x3b, 256k1->0x3b, 192k1->0x554123b
   224k1 and 256k1 share the SAME high byte 0x3b (and the same V): reuse, not a
   per-curve counter. 192k1's 0x554123b ends in 0x3b too, with 0x55412 above it.
   So 0x3b is a shared constant on three curves; 0x55412 is extra on 192k1 only.
   Candidate sources for 0x3b = 59 and 0x554123b = 89395771 / 0x55412 = 349202:
     secp160k1: p mod 2^32=0xffffac73, n mod 2^32=0xca16b6b3, 2^f - p (Mersenne offset)=0x10000538d, p+1-n (trace)=-0x1b8fa16dfab9bca170a3f, b=0x7
     secp192k1: p mod 2^32=0xffffee37, n mod 2^32=0x74defd8d, 2^f - p (Mersenne offset)=0x1000011c9, b=0x3
     secp224k1: p mod 2^32=0xffffe56d, n mod 2^32=0x769fb1f7, 2^f - p (Mersenne offset)=0x100001a93, p+1-n (trace)=-0x1dce8d2ec6184caf0a972769fcc89, b=0x5

7. The 32-bit-word layout, and the 'uninitialised top limb' reading
   Big-endian 32-bit words (word0 = most significant):
     secp160k1  48ce563f 89a0ed94 14f5aa28 ad0d96d6 795f9c62
     secp192k1  0554123b 78ce563f 89a0ed94 14f5aa28 ad0d96d6 795f9c66
     secp224k1  0000003b 78ce563f 89a0ed94 14f5aa28 ad0d96d6 795f9c63
     secp256k1  0000003b 78ce563f 89a0ed94 14f5aa28 ad0d96d6 795f9c63
   The low five words are the block [78ce563f 89a0ed94 14f5aa28 ad0d96d6
   795f9c6?]; the sixth word is 0x0000003b (224/256), 0x0554123b (192), or
   absent (160k1, whose top word's high byte is 0x48 not 0x78).
   If the sixth word were uninitialised buffer or a free-running counter it
   would differ between runs; instead secp224k1 and secp256k1 carry the
   identical 0x3b and share it as the low byte of secp192k1's word. A value
   that repeats across independent curve generations is assigned, not leftover.

SUMMARY
  * One 160-bit block V = 78ce563f89a0ed9414f5aa28ad0d96d6795f9c6? underlies all
    four; its middle 152 bits are fixed, its top and bottom nibbles are soft.
  * secp224k1 and secp256k1 are byte-identical at x(G/2): V, high byte 0x3b,
    last nibble 3. A chosen x reused verbatim across two fields.
  * The high material above V is 0x3b on 192/224/256 (0x55412 extra on 192) and
    absent on 160k1, which instead has 0x30 cleared from V's top byte.
  * Excluded for the high material: FIPS/X9.62 expansion (single-seed
    contradiction + weight), field-size truncation (192 > 224/256 in prefix),
    a per-curve counter (224=256), a date, and a function of p/n/trace/b.
  * The top-byte 0x30 clear is a deliberate 2-bit edit, not forced by the field.
  * Bet: the high bytes are small hand-assigned tags written into the top limb
    of a fixed-width buffer holding V, not a computed quantity. 0x3b (=';'=59)
    shared by three curves is the single sub-constant most worth identifying.
